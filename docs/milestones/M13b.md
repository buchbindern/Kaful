# M13b. The water_filter → boiler scaling link

## The problem it addressed
The eversys boilers scaled at a constant rate, whatever the water filter was doing. The manual says otherwise: W-014
(p114, label 114) reads "The water filter has to be changed after its life time cycle. Otherwise the boilers will be
damaged due to the limescale." M13b adds couplings between components: a typed connection in the twin, a coupling
form in the templates and the render, a strength prior with honest provenance, verify checks, and the estimators
treating a coupled group jointly. The manual states that the link exists, not how strong it is, so the strength is a
wide, flagged default.

As in M12 and M13, the data is synthetic: a **pipeline sanity check, not validation**.

## What was built
- **Coupling form** (`kaful/templates/couplings.py`, pure numpy): `hardness_passthrough`. The boiler's rate is
  `k_b · m(x)`, with x = clip(D_filter / D_limit_filter, 0, 1) and `m(x) = (r + (1 − r) x) / ((1 + r) / 2)`.
  - r is the hardness fraction a fresh filter passes (bypass and leakage).
  - m averages exactly 1 over a filter cycle renewed at W-014, so k = D_limit / life keeps its meaning: life in
    normal service (decided 2026-11-16).
  - r = 1 means no coupling. Past its limit the filter passes everything (m(1) = 2 / (1 + r)).
  - The form also provides a closed-form crossing time, a `delayed_mean` for the what-if, and a linear basis
    (m = w0(r) · 1 + w1(r) · x) for the estimator.
  - Hypothesis property tests cover m ≥ 0, monotone in x, x clipped, a cycle mean of 1 for every r, no coupling at
    r = 1, linearity in the basis, and the crossing time against numerical integration.
- **Formats** (approved 2026-11-16):
  - TwinDefinition schema 4: a `Connection` has a from-binding and a to-binding, a form, its parameters, and
    provenance for the link's existence (`manual` when its quote is found on the page, else a flagged
    `deployment`).
  - The parameter role `coupling`.
  - machine.toml `[[connection]]`.
  - `OperatingProfile.renew_at`, the what-if. It is written only when set, so existing profiles and forecasts keep
    their bytes.
- **Parameterize** (step `g_coupling_priors`): `water_filter.residual_fraction` ~ Uniform(0, 1), source `default`,
  confidence 0 (computed: the share of the admissible range the prior excludes). Three flags:
  - on the filter: "no documented value";
  - on each boiler: "rate depends on water_filter";
  - on each boiler: its life was judged without the coupling in the prompt. The cached coffee_boiler judgement
    assumed "the water filter is past its rated life"; the steam_boiler one assumed "normal service". Both are read
    as normal service.
- **Build** (step `e_bind_connections`): the declared connection is bound and its quote checked against the page.
  Understand found no water_filter → boiler flow path, so the link is declared.
- **Render**: a target's rate is multiplied by `m(x_source)`, with x read at the start of each step. Rendering a
  target without its source is an error. `Dynamics.rates` now receives the damage. Before, it assumed every D = 0,
  which was harmless while templates were state-free but would have mis-scaled a coupled target's process noise.
- **Verify**:
  - `connection_endpoints`: no self-links, no cycles.
  - `connection_form`: a known version; slots bound to `coupling` parameters whose prior lies inside the slot's
    bounds.
  - `connection_units`: the source's D and D_limit share a unit; the slot dimension is checked.
  - `life_consistency` is unchanged. For a coupled twin it reads a `nominal_uncoupled@q0.5` run, with every
    coupling neutral.
  - The new `coupled_life_consistency` holds a target to the closed-form crossing time, within the same ±1% band.
  - Dependents of a quarantined part (logged in M11): a quarantined filter quarantines both boilers; a quarantined
    boiler does not quarantine the filter.
- **Estimation**:
  - A connection joins groups as a shared channel does, so eversys has one observed group (coffee_boiler,
    steam_boiler, top_piston, water_filter).
  - **Unread bindings** (`kaful/estimation/unread.py`, decided 2026-11-17): a binding that no observation's
    likelihood depends on is not carried through resampling. The split is structural: observed bindings plus their
    upstream sources are read, and anything else in the group is unread.
  - The filter steps only the read bindings and keeps, per particle, the integrals of the form's basis functions
    along the source's path, restarted at the target's scheduled replaces.
  - Each export draws the unread parameters from the prior and the damage as D = k·S + √(ρ²·L·k·S)·z, where S is
    the w-weighted sum of the integrals. This is exact in distribution for the Wiener damage model given the path
    (tested against a forward simulation of the whole group).
  - A binding that doesn't qualify stays simulated, with the reason recorded: several incoming connections, a
    clean or descale rule, an operating input, or it feeds something.
- **What-if** (`kaful estimate whatif <machine> --renew-at water_filter=1.5`): forecasts from the same belief with
  the same seed under the documented profile and the what-if. Three views (new machine, per unit and issue, long
  run), each with a computed one-line conclusion, plus a note on what information would narrow the result.
- **Scoring**: parts joined only through a connection get three checks: a posterior = prior KS test
  (`settings.scoring`), the forecast width against open-loop (25–75%, since the boilers' 95% points are mostly
  censored), and how much of the time-to-limit spread each carried parameter explains.

**What-if** (`outputs/whatif/20261117T185055Z-58ce82__water_filter_x1.5.json`, water_filter replaced at 1.5x its
W-014 point):
- **New machine:** coffee_boiler's median time to limit goes from 7,540 to 7,292 products. Only 27% of samples
  move; the others reach the limit before the filter is first left past W-014. Those that move do so by −7% at the
  median (−23% to 0%), and r explains 74% of the change's spread. steam_boiler goes from 1,809 to 1,789: 9% of
  samples move, by −5% (−25% to 0%), and r explains 80%.
- **Synthetic units:** in the median (unit, issue) forecast, 14% of coffee_boiler samples move and 46% have
  already reached the limit; for steam_boiler, 2% move and 91% have reached it.
- **Long run:** the boilers' scaling rate rises by +11% (90%: +1% to +30%), from r alone.
- **What would narrow it:** r, from a filter datasheet (its bypass setting) or a water-hardness test downstream of a
  fresh filter. Knowing r to ±0.1 narrows the long-run change to +8%..+14%. Only boiler data (a descaling record,
  scale inspection) would narrow the boilers' own k.

**Exit criteria:**
1. The coupling form's property tests pass.
2. Eversys verify passes with the new checks; the other machines' twins are unchanged apart from
   `schema_version`. (tiny_pump is a test fixture, rebuilt by the tests.)
3. The synthetic observations are byte-identical to M12's.
4. The particle filter still beats open-loop on top_piston and water_filter, and its results are unchanged within
   Monte Carlo noise.
5. The boilers' k and r posteriors equal their priors: KS as written, 0 rejections in 20 units for each. The
   coffee_boiler state error is no worse than open-loop's.
6. Determinism: run 58ce82 (`PYTHONHASHSEED` 1, 6 workers) and run ab22e0 (`PYTHONHASHSEED` 0, 1 worker) give 241
   files each. 200 are byte-identical; the other 41 JSONs differ only in run ids and the sha256 of files that embed
   them. This is the eversys double-run logged under M13.
7. The what-if is reported with a conclusion per view and the note on r.

## Deferred and logged
- **steam_boiler** (logged in PLAN): it fails inside the synthetic window in 19 of 20 units. That comes from its life
  prior (median ~1,158 products in normal service, implausibly short) and the lack of a descaling rule, not from the
  link. Options: a re-judged life (paid, small), or a `[[param]]` from someone with Eversys experience.
- The linear breakthrough is an assumption: an ion-exchange filter's output hardness stays low for most of its
  capacity and rises near the end. No document gives the curve.
- Understand could find couplings itself (a flow-path or "protects" relation); today they are declared.
- A `[[param]]` override of a coupling strength needs a bounded prior form (DeclaredParam gives a lognormal, which
  would fail `connection_form` for r).
- Unread bindings with a clean or descale rule, several incoming connections, or operating inputs stay simulated (no
  sufficient statistic is implemented for them).
- Partial run directories from interrupted sessions: 20261116T211243Z-a3192b and 20261117T181003Z-5dbda3
  (manifests left `running`). A `kaful runs prune` is logged under M13.

## Surprises
- **The boilers' posterior was not their prior, though no data reads them.** Every W-014 report sharpens the filter's
  parameters, a resample keeps a few dozen ancestors, and the boilers' parameters came along as a clump of those few
  prior draws. The KS test rejected "posterior = prior" in 9–11 of 20 units, while the medians stayed unbiased and
  uncorrelated with the truth. Rebuilding the unread bindings at export removes this exactly.
- **A line-break hyphen hid the quote.** The page has "lime-\nscale", which the matcher joins into "limescale"; a quote
  copying "lime- scale" did not match.
- **W-014 says almost nothing about the boilers.** It fixes the filter's phase, and m varies only between 0.67 and
  1.33 at the median r, while a boiler's own k spans about 7× across its 90% interval.
