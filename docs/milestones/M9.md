# M9. Parameterize

## The problem it addressed
The legacy twins used literal constants for rates and thresholds, with no record of where the numbers came
from, and the time unit was mixed (hours against brew cycles). M9 adds the parameterize phase (p4). It turns
each full-model component into parameters, every one of them a prior distribution with a source. Rates are
calibrated from documented lives where the documents state one, and come from a wide, labelled LLM judgement
where they don't. The reports show how much of the uncertainty in remaining useful life (RUL) each prior
carries.

## What was built
- **Template protocol and three families** (`kaful/templates/families.py`), plain numpy, using the M4
  design.
  - Each template tracks damage D, with a unit, up to D_limit. Health = 1 − D/D_limit is derived.
  - Every family has the normalised form dD/dt = k · f(conditions), with f(nominal) = 1. So
    k = D_limit / life, and the life at any other conditions follows in closed form.
  - **accumulation:** f = (load/load_nom)^n, for clogging, scaling, residue, exhaustion and wear.
  - **arrhenius:** f = exp(Ea/R · (1/T_nom − 1/T)), for thermal ageing and overheating.
  - **bearing_fatigue:** f = (P/P_nom)^p · (n/n_nom), from ISO 281.
  - A replacement resets D to 0.
  - Property tests use hypothesis 6.135.0 (test-only, pinned). They check:
    - the protocol;
    - rate ≥ 0, and monotone in each condition;
    - zero load gives zero wear;
    - health starts at 1.0, stays in [0, 1] and never rises;
    - simulating at nominal reaches D_limit at the calibrated life to within 1%.
- **Steps** (`kaful/phases/p4_parameterize/`):
  - **a_choose_templates:** one family per full-model component, chosen from its fault modes' mechanism
    words, strongest family first. The component's other modes are listed as not separately modelled.
  - **b_read_life_statements:** reads each life signal from scope back off its page. A schedule row gives
    a number under a "Running hours" header, a period word, or a period by ditto; a sentence gives a number
    and unit. Each statement gets a value, a unit, a kind and a consumable mark.
    - Inspection rows (check, clean, inspect) are recorded but bound nothing.
    - Only a verbatim quote counts as `manual`.
    - A row from a scanned page's transcription is used with a wider prior and flagged.
    - A quote missing from a page that has a text layer is recorded but not used.
  - **c_life_priors:** a lognormal prior on nominal life T from the binding statement.
    - Interval rules, as approved: consumable replacement q 0.20, σ 0.35; other replacement q 0.05, σ 0.6;
      service q 0.05, σ 0.8; machine-wide q 0.05, σ 1.0.
    - A rated life uses an L10 Weibull with β 1.5.
    - A calendar-only interval is converted through a flagged default duty cycle.
    - k = D_limit / T.
  - **d_judge_lives:** `parameterize.life_judgement` for components with no usable documented life. It is
    the only LLM step.
    - Three samples per component, pooled, with σ held at 1.2 or more. Confidence is computed by code from
      how much the samples agree.
    - A life stated as an amount processed (for example litres) is converted to the time unit through
      `[[spec]]` or a flagged default (water per product: 0.12 L, σ 0.7). D then has that amount's unit.
  - **e_value_roles:**
    - documented channel limits become thresholds: `spec` as a point, `setting_range` as uniform, an
      example never;
    - Ea comes from a wide literature prior;
    - the ISO 281 exponent p is 3 for ball and 10/3 for roller bearings;
    - `[[spec]]` sets template inputs;
    - `[[param]]` replaces any prior and recomputes k.
  - **f_check:** the gates. A failure fails the phase:
    - a unit that won't convert;
    - P(T < interval) > 0.25 under the component's prior, which catches a point life below a documented
      interval;
    - a parameter resting on an example value;
    - a `[[param]]` with no such parameter or the wrong unit.
- **Artifacts and config:**
  - ParamSet schema 2: per-component templates, a `life` role, and life statements that carry their
    basis, the value in the time unit, `consumable`, `usable` and `transcribed`.
  - machine.toml gains `[[spec]]` and `[[param]]`. The manifest records every `[[param]]` as an override.
  - Time units confirmed: eversys = product, air_compressor = hour, CNC = hour, tiny_pump = hour.
- **Report** (`kaful parameterize report <machine>`, saved to `outputs/param_reports/<run>.json/.md`):
  - counts by source;
  - RUL by profile (nominal, load +20%, speed +20%, temperature +10 K), with the reason whenever a profile
    can't be evaluated;
  - each prior's share of the spread in log RUL, and its effect on median RUL at −1σ and +1σ;
  - the interval assumption, with the median under every rule;
  - judged lives first;
  - a machine-level flag when the whole model rests on judged lives.
- **Review:** the onboarding review adds a Parameters section (judged lives first) and `param` entries.
  `review-apply` writes them to `[[param]]`.
- **Eval framework:** a `covers` compare, which checks that a judged range contains the hidden values after
  unit conversion, and a `ratio_at_most` check for sharpness.
- `kaful/doctext.py`: text helpers now shared by scope and parameterize, moved out of `p3_scope/helpers`.
- `kaful.toml` pins `parameterize.life_judgement` to Haiku 5.5.

## Decisions (Natalie, 2026-11-09/10)
- The template protocol and the three families are built in M9; one template per component.
- Templates keep the M4 damage-with-units design.
- Interval prior rules as proposed, shown as assumptions in the report.
- Time units as above.
- The bearings use life_judgement until load ratings are declared via `[[spec]]` (flagged).
- life_judgement approved with a $0.10 cap. Sharpness was added to its eval: each case's 95th/5th
  percentile ratio must be at most 100.
- No duty cycles were declared, so the wide defaults are used, flagged.
- air_dryer was overridden to connection_only via `[[scope]]`, through the real review round trip.
- No `[[param]]` corrections: the judged priors stay wide and flagged, for data to narrow.

## Results
Parameterize runs on life_judgement v2, all passed: eversys 1d5c29, air_compressor 31a709, CNC 303782.

| Machine | Full models | Life from documents | Life judged | Parameters by source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| eversys (products) | 6 | 0 | 6 | llm_judgement 12, default 6 |
| air_compressor (hours) | 5 | 3 (consumable replacements, p29 label 27) | 2 | calibrated 6, default 5, llm_judgement 4, literature 1 |
| CNC (hours) | 3 | 0 (bearings: L10 model, no ratings) | 3 | llm_judgement 6, default 3, standard 2, literature 1 |
| tiny_pump (hours, offline test) | 2 | 0 (naming gap, below) | 2 | |

- **air_compressor documented lives** (median, 5–95%):
  - air filter element and oil: 5,370 h (3,020–9,550), from "4000 … Replace …";
  - oil separator element: 10,740 h (6,040–19,100), from "2-yearly 8000 … Have oil separator (OS) replaced".
- **eversys water_filter used the litres → products conversion:** a judged capacity of 1,669 L (σ 1.2) at the
  default 0.12 L per product gives 13,900 products (σ 1.39). D is in litres, and k is the water per product.
  The checkpoint 4 report said this conversion was not triggered; that was wrong (corrected 2026-11-10, M10).
- **Judged lives** (median): CNC spindle_motor 3,100 h, angular-contact bearings 10,200 h, cylindrical
  roller bearings 16,200 h. eversys water_filter 13,900, coffee_boiler 6,300, top_piston 5,600,
  steam_boiler 1,130, steam_wand 940 and milk_system 91 products. air_cooler 6,100 h, oil_cooler 8,700 h.
  All of them have σ ≥ 1.2 and are flagged.
- **What drives RUL:** the life prior (through k) explains about 100% of RUL spread everywhere; Ea and p
  show about 0%. The bearings respond to load (RUL ÷ 1.2³ at +20%). Arrhenius components can't be
  evaluated at +10 K, because no nominal temperature is documented or declared.
- **life_judgement evals:** v1 and v2 each passed twice. Every run scored covers 0.9 and sharp 1.0, with
  widths from 6 to 30. CDV's heating element covers only one of its two values (5 and 15 years) in every
  run. The air_compressor cases are scored and were never tuned on.
- **Gates:** no failures on the real machines. Unit tests cover each gate.
- `pytest -q`: 681 passed, offline. mypy is clean on `kaful/`. Two hash seeds give the same ParamSet.

## Cost
About $0.015 in total, under the $0.10 cap:
- life_judgement v1 evals $0.0028;
- v1 parameterize runs $0.0049, including the eversys run that failed;
- v2 evals $0.0026;
- v2 parameterize runs $0.0045.

A parameterize run costs at most $0.003 per machine, and $0 when no component needs judging.

## Surprises
- **Most lives aren't documented.** Only 3 of the 14 full-model components have a usable life statement.
  CNC and eversys rest entirely on judged lives, and the report and review flag this.
- **The v1 failure on eversys.** One life_judgement reply hit `max_tokens` after about 100 visible tokens;
  internal reasoning used up the 800-token limit. That reply also wrote its unit as "litres of water
  heated". v2 fixed both problems: max_tokens 4000, and the unit field holds a unit only.
- **No document value feeds value roles yet.** Understand stored no channel ranges or product settings on
  the dev machines, so there are no documented thresholds or nominal inputs, and no nominal temperature for
  Arrhenius. eversys prints its water quantity per product (p72, label 72: "0 to 1000 ticks … approximately
  equal to ml"), but no artifact holds it.
- **The tiny_pump naming gap.** "Replace the cartridge every 2000 operating hours" doesn't name "inlet
  cartridge filter (F1)" as printed, so scope attaches no life and the filter's life is judged.
- **Two classification rules were fixed after checkpoint 2's first run.** Inspection words now win over
  "service" ("Check air filter service indicator"). A consumable word may now end a name word
  ("Roto-injectfluid").
